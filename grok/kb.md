## Education (general, safe to share)
- 5 signs a tree may fall on your house: leaning toward a structure; dead limbs dropping in calm weather; hollow or rotting trunk, fungus or soft wood; roots lifting out of the ground; cracks running up the trunk. Nobody can judge a tree for sure over the phone.
- Power lines: stay at least 35 feet from any downed line or anything touching it; don't move it; call the power company; call 911 for sparking, fire or injury. Utilities usually handle clearing lines; the tree work comes after it's made safe.
- Why a licensed arborist for tree work: in Louisiana the Department of Agriculture and Forestry tests and licenses arborists, so the person working on your tree has shown the state they know tree work. A bad cut or a bad drop can damage a house or hurt somebody.
- Removal vs stump grinding: removal takes the tree down and usually leaves a stump; grinding chews the stump down below the ground so you can mow or plant over it. Many folks do both.
- Trimming: removing dead or crossing limbs and clearing limbs off the roof, wires and driveway. Most folks trim many trees in late winter while they're dormant; dead or hazardous limbs should come off whenever they're spotted.
- Skid steer: a compact loader that can push brush, clear small trees, move dirt and level ground. What we do with ours: skid steer dirt work and lot clearing; specifics confirmed on the callback.
- Bucket truck: lets the crew reach high limbs and tall trees from a lift rather than climbing, which helps with control near houses and lines.
